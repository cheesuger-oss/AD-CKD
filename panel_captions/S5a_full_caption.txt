S5a, Primary creatinine-based eGFR Mendelian-randomisation diagnostics. A, Estimates and 95% confidence intervals from inverse-variance weighted, MR-Egger, weighted-median and weighted-mode analyses using 264 instruments. B, Leave-one-out inverse-variance weighted estimates; the orange line is the full estimate. The confidence intervals are wide and the omitted-instrument analyses are not independent estimates. C, Single-instrument ratio estimates against inverse standard error. This is a descriptive precision plot and not a formal test proving absence of directional pleiotropy. S5b, Exploratory thoracic-aortic-aneurysm outcomes from FinnGen and CHIP-MGI. A, Complete method estimates and 95% confidence intervals. B, Frozen outcome identity, harmonised-instrument counts and F-statistic summaries. These outcomes were exploratory and do not replace the prespecified aortic-dissection outcome or upgrade the renal-to-dissection causal claim. S5c, Detailed genetic triangulation. A, Univariate observed-scale SNP-heritability estimates and quality-control status. Aortic-dissection heritability did not reach the prespecified precision gate (Z=2.40<4), so bivariate genetic correlation was not estimated; this does not show absence of shared genetic architecture. B, Chromosome 14 PP.H4 across the frozen shared-variant priors. The result is prior-sensitive, and eGFRcys and serum cystatin C are not independent replications because their source samples overlap. C, Chromosome 20 PP.H3 and PP.H4 under the primary-prior regional model; H3 favours distinct variants only under that fitted single-causal-variant model in this region. D, Reverse-MR estimates, confidence intervals, BH q values and heterogeneity P values. Five estimable outcomes each used two aortic-dissection instruments; serum cystatin C retained one instrument and was underidentified. Effect scales differ across renal outcomes, and two instruments do not permit robust pleiotropy sensitivity analyses. Collectively, these diagnostics constrain interpretation but do not prove absence of a causal effect.
