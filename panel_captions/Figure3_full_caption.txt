A, Primary inverse-variance weighted estimates and 95% confidence intervals for nine prespecified genetically proxied renal phenotypes and FinnGen release 13 aortic dissection. Odds ratios are shown on a logarithmic scale, and the number of harmonised instruments is shown for each phenotype. Benjamini–Hochberg correction was applied across the nine primary estimates; none passed correction (all q=0.685). Rapid3 and CKDi25 retained only two instruments. Exposure definitions and scales differ, so effect sizes should not be compared directly across phenotypes. B, Conditional multivariable Mendelian-randomisation estimates and 95% confidence intervals for eGFRcrea and systolic blood pressure. These are mutually adjusted effects under the fitted model and do not test whether blood pressure mediates an effect of renal function. Estimates retain their exposure-specific native scales, and their magnitudes should not be compared directly. Conditional F statistics used a zero exposure-covariance approximation because the covariance was unavailable; exposure-sample overlap limits this diagnostic. C, Evidential status of complementary genetic analyses. Bivariate LD-score regression was not performed because aortic-dissection SNP heritability did not meet the prespecified precision threshold (Z=2.40<4); this is inconclusive rather than evidence of absent shared genetic architecture. Chromosome 14 colocalisation support for eGFRcys and serum cystatin C was sensitive to the shared-variant prior, and the overlapping source samples do not constitute independent replication. At chromosome 20, the fitted primary-prior model favoured distinct causal variants (H3) in that region. Reverse Mendelian randomisation retained two aortic-dissection instruments for five estimable renal outcomes; none passed correction, and serum cystatin C was underidentified with one instrument. These analyses do not prove absence of a causal effect.
